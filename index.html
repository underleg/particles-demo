Skull emitters (from Particlr's CC0 "Portal" pull layer) · PixiJS v8 · @particlr/runtime · Spine 4.2

Three setups, one emitter each, picked from the menu; seed 77, normal blend. Each is a Particlr file, public/effects/<setup>.prt, with the skull sheet embedded; the app sets the count, the screen-relative positions and the per-step behaviour (Swirl's sway and second attractor, Cascade's floor spin) on top. Only the shown setup is simulated. # particles sets how many skulls are alive at once; changing it rebuilds the emitter, because a Particlr pool's size is fixed when its effect is built.
  • Emitter: Cascade — # particles alive at once (the cap; births run 50% ahead of deaths to keep it full) 56–80 px skulls fired upward in a 30° fan at 300–360 px/s, under 450 px/s² gravity; they land on a floor 80 px below the emitter and bounce (55% vertical speed kept, 80% sideways); 2.5–3.5 s life, growing from half size to full; fades in over the first 15% of life, holds at 90% opacity, then fades out only in the last 179–250 ms (the gradient is per life fraction, so the exact time depends on each skull's lifetime). Each skull has a random tilt (±180°) and faces left or right at random (50% mirrored). It doesn't spin in the air; each time it hits the floor it takes a new random spin, 90–360 °/s either way (set by the host, as Particlr's collision can't change spin). Drawn oldest in front, so the large landed skulls cover the small rising ones.
  • Emitter: Birds — exactly # particles skulls flying left to right in a 300 px band, entering and leaving off screen (a kill zone just past the right edge removes each one, and a new one enters on the left). Speed is random, 40–120 px/s, and size follows it (bySpeed: 32–90 px), so smaller birds are slower and drawn further back. All mirrored to face their flight; no tilt or fade. Simulated 40 s ahead at startup so the flock already spans the screen.
  • Emitter: Swirl — the cascade's fountain fired from lower down (85% of the way down the open band), its emitter swinging left and right on a 2 s sine wave (±25% of the width, so from under one attractor to under the other; y fixed), with no gravity or floor, pulled by two attractors at (25%, 50%) and (75%, 50%) of the band. Each pulls straight at its point at up to 3,000 px/s², easing smoothly to nothing at a radius of 1.2× its distance from the emitter. One is the layer's own attractor and the other is Particlr's host attractor; the two use the same force formula, so they match. Mild drag (1) lets skulls spiral in; without it they swing straight through the points and out again. Simulated in world space, so moving the emitter leaves skulls already in flight where they are.
  • Skulls: Spine — a live Spine skeleton per particle, posed each frame from the particle's age. Offered up to 1,000 particles: at ~2 ms per 250 skeletons, 10,000 would run at a few fps. # particles stops at 10,000 because Particlr caps a single emitter there.
  • Skulls: Flipbook — the 'still' loop (1.28 s) as an 8×5 sheet of 96 px frames (~31 fps), embedded in each .prt file and drawn by particlr itself.
Deterministic: Effect.step(dt) per Pixi tick, then PixiParticleRenderer.sync().
